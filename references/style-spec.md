# Visual Style Specification

## One-Sentence Formula

Build a sunlit luxury editorial world in which a product belongs to an unhurried coastal ritual: 60% tactile lifestyle atmosphere, 25% unmistakable product or brand story, and 15% graphic color cue.

The signature is not generic "vacation mood." It is the tension between relaxed human behavior and tightly controlled commercial art direction.

## Four Canonical Modes

### 1. Lifestyle Hero

A person performs a small leisure ritual near a pool, terrace, or shoreline while the product remains a clear foreground or lower-third hero.

- Crop identity rather than centering a face; sunglasses, a book, a hat, hands, shoulders, or a torso can carry the human story.
- Let the body create a diagonal and the environment provide depth.
- Keep the product physically grounded on stone, linen, wood, or glass. It should occupy roughly 10-22% of the frame, large enough to read at thumbnail size.
- Use two or three narrative props at most: fruit, a cocktail, a magazine, sunglasses, jewelry, or a striped textile.

### 2. Editorial Still Life

A 30-45 degree overhead composition combines the product with one culturally suggestive object, such as a record player, book, tray, or newspaper.

- Use a strong diagonal beam of sunlight and visible surface grain.
- Establish one large anchor, one medium product group, and one or two small outliers.
- Repeat the brand color in three places to make the frame feel designed rather than decorated.
- Leave a plausible path for the eye; avoid a perfectly centered ecommerce flat lay.

### 3. Architectural Poster Wall

A 2x3 campaign artwork is mounted or projected onto a warm stone facade, with shallow architectural details and direct sunlight.

- Build the six tiles independently, then assemble them into one exact rectangle.
- Alternate product, human detail, texture, and one graphic tile.
- Keep gutters even and outer edges perfectly aligned.
- Add the brand wordmark below the grid with a real layout tool. Use the finished artwork as one planar asset when placing it onto the wall.

### 4. Campaign Mosaic

A full-frame 3x3 grid mixes environment, person, product detail, props, and a center title card.

- Use eight image tiles plus one centered brand tile.
- Distribute human, object, and atmosphere tiles so no adjacent row repeats the same subject type three times.
- Carry one sky/water color and one warm accent through at least five tiles.
- Treat the grid as a single cover, not a contact sheet of unrelated images.

## Visual DNA

### Mood

- Aspirational but unhurried
- Sensual through light and material, not overt posing
- Commercially polished but not sterile
- Contemporary European summer with a faint 1970s editorial memory

### Setting And Materials

Prefer limestone, pale stucco, oak parquet, walnut, pool water, blue sky, linen, straw, ribbed glass, glossy fruit, polished metal, vinyl, and striped textiles. Combine one architectural material, one soft material, and one reflective or wet surface.

Avoid generic hotel-lobby luxury, nightclub darkness, tropical clutter, excessive flowers, and empty beige minimalism.

### Light

- Hard late-morning or late-afternoon sun at roughly 35-50 degrees
- Warm direct light with cool sky or water bounce
- Shadows should be visibly shaped, not crushed; highlights should stay textured
- Slight filmic grain and crisp microcontrast, without HDR halos
- No foggy backlight, heavy diffusion, or synthetic orange-and-teal grading

Choose one sun direction and preserve it across a series. The object, person, wall, and contact shadow must agree.

### Camera

- 35 mm environmental editorial view or 50 mm product-lifestyle view
- Moderate depth of field, approximately an f/5.6-f/8 visual feel
- Eye level, table level, or a controlled 30-45 degree overhead view
- Crisp product edges and readable material texture

Avoid ultrawide distortion, extreme top-down symmetry, macro-only storytelling, and f/1.2 blur that erases the location.

## Palette System

Do not use every swatch. Choose one warm neutral, two atmosphere colors, and one anchor color.

| Role | Color | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm white | Sun-bleached ivory | `#F4EFE7` | Swimwear, paper, negative space |
| Architectural neutral | Limestone | `#D8CCBC` | Walls, tables, matte grounds |
| Warm material | Sunlit oak | `#B87543` | Wood, skin-adjacent warmth |
| Coastal cool | Hazy sky | `#AFCADC` | Sky, center cards, stripes |
| Water accent | Pool aqua | `#69BFC0` | Water, glass reflection, small props |
| Soft vivid | Bougainvillea | `#D95E7C` | Cosmetics, fabric, campaign graphics |
| Deep anchor | Oxblood | `#8F3433` | Packaging, ribbon, jewelry cue |
| Botanical support | Sage | `#7F9375` | Fruit, foliage, fragrance cues |
| Text neutral | Warm ink | `#282421` | Editorial typography |

Target distribution: 55-65% warm neutral, 20-30% sky/water/material color, and 10-15% anchor color. Repeat the anchor color at three spatial points. Keep whites warm but clean; do not wash the whole frame in beige.

## Styling Grammar

- Product first, lifestyle second: the setting explains desire, but the product explains the frame.
- One hero, one cultural prop, one sensory prop, one material ground is usually enough.
- Use edible cues selectively: figs, cherries, tomatoes, citrus, or a pale cocktail. Pick one family, not a fruit basket.
- Human styling favors white, narrow stripes, restrained gold jewelry, warm skin, glossy lips, and clean nails.
- Let one object cross a grid edge or frame boundary for energy, but keep the product fully legible.
- Use negative space as a breathing zone, not as the main aesthetic.

## Typography And Graphics

- Add critical text with a layout tool after image generation.
- Pair an elegant high-contrast serif for a brand or campaign title with a clean grotesk for one graphic tile.
- Use one short title or wordmark. Avoid body copy inside the visual.
- Keep letter spacing at 0 unless a supplied brand system requires otherwise.
- Match text color to warm ink, ivory, oxblood, or a sampled brand color.
- Never accept misspelled AI lettering on a pack, poster, book, or record label.

## Optional Carousel Arc

When the user requests a series, use this as a flexible sequence rather than a mandatory count:

1. Cover: strongest lifestyle hero or campaign mosaic
2. Product still life: clear commercial read
3. Human ritual: hand, lips, torso, or accessory in use
4. Environment: pool, sea, terrace, or interior
5. Macro material: formula, texture, stone, fruit, or jewelry detail
6. Graphic interlude: poster wall or title tile
7. Closing still life: simple product and recurring prop

## Quality Rubric

Score the finished visual before delivery.

| Dimension | Points | Pass condition |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Light logic | 20 | Sun direction, contact shadow, reflection, and warmth agree |
| Composition | 20 | One immediate hero; diagonals and negative space feel intentional |
| Product fidelity | 20 | Shape, label, scale, color, and placement are credible |
| Palette | 15 | Warm neutral base, coastal counterpoint, and one repeated anchor |
| Material tactility | 15 | Stone, wood, skin, water, fabric, and glass have distinct texture |
| Editorial restraint | 10 | No excess props, fake text, UI chrome, or visual clutter |

Pass at 80/100 or higher. Any obvious hand deformity, floating product, warped pack, incorrect logo, unreadable generated text, or broken grid geometry is an automatic revision regardless of score.

## Failure Patterns

- Generic resort stock photo with no product motive
- Centered ecommerce packshot floating over a decorative background
- Monochrome beige, muddy highlights, or overdone teal-and-orange color grade
- Too many tropical props, flowers, fruits, or jewelry pieces
- Model-like posing and direct eye contact that shift the image toward influencer portraiture
- Random collage tiles with inconsistent sun, palette, lens, or product scale
- Brand logos or packaging redrawn by a generator when real assets are available
- Screenshots that include Xiaohongshu UI when only the underlying visual was requested
